\chapter*{中文摘要}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{中文摘要}
精神科初診逐字稿中的鑑別診斷，需要在證據不完整下比較多個可能疾病，而非只辨識一個標籤。醫師需整合症狀、病程、時序、功能影響、排除病因與共病，但一次晤談未必能取得所有必要資訊。大型語言模型能整理候選診斷與證據，但流暢輸出不代表候選完整、準則判斷有逐字稿支持，或主診斷選擇恰當。只看最終準確率，也無法判斷分歧出現在哪個階段。

本研究提出 HiED，一套用於中文精神科逐字稿的混合式、以證據為基礎的多代理框架。診斷路徑透過相似案例檢索產生 Top-3 與初步主診斷；準則核對路徑使用 14 個診斷類別專屬的 Criterion Checker，將準則判定為符合、不符合或資訊不足，並形成準則相容集合。HiED 保存這些輸出，分別評估候選覆蓋、準則相容與主診斷選擇。

在固定的 1,000 例 LingxiDiag-16K 內部保留測試集上，HiED 的 Top-1 Accuracy 為 51.8\%，Top-3 Accuracy 為 80.2\%。其 Top-1 與 Single LLM 幾乎相同；TF--IDF 加邏輯迴歸在同來源資料上較強，但跨來源時明顯下降。最大已記錄的基準分歧類型為參考診斷已進入 Top-3 並通過準則核對，卻未被選為最終主診斷；在可進行準則分析的案例中，此類型占內部案例的 29.7\%，在 MDD-5k 中占 25.6\%。此模式支持主診斷選擇可能是瓶頸的假設，但不能證明 finalization 單獨造成分歧。所測試的主診斷選擇方法均未明確優於 Direct-Answer。

HiED 並未證明最終診斷準確率較高。其主要貢獻是提出可稽核的雙路徑架構與分階段評估框架，使候選遺漏、準則不相容、資訊不足與主診斷選擇分歧能在同一病例中分別檢視。所有結果皆來自合成中文逐字稿，不構成臨床驗證。

\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
Psychiatric differential diagnosis from a first-interview transcript requires comparing plausible disorders under incomplete evidence rather than assigning one label. Clinicians must integrate symptoms, course, timing, impairment, exclusionary causes, and comorbidity, but one interview may not provide all necessary information. Large language models can organize candidate diagnoses and evidence, but fluent output does not guarantee complete candidates, transcript-grounded criterion judgments, or appropriate primary-diagnosis selection. Final accuracy alone cannot show where disagreement appears.

This thesis proposes HiED, a hybrid, evidence-grounded multi-agent framework for Chinese psychiatric transcripts. The diagnosis path retrieves similar cases and produces a Top-3 list and a proposed primary diagnosis. The criterion-checking path uses fourteen diagnosis-specific Criterion Checkers to label criteria as met, not met, or insufficient evidence and form a criterion-compatible set. HiED preserves these outputs and separately evaluates candidate coverage, criterion compatibility, and primary-diagnosis selection.

On a fixed 1,000-case LingxiDiag-16K held-out set, HiED achieved 51.8\% Top-1 Accuracy and 80.2\% Top-3 Accuracy. Its Top-1 was almost identical to that of the Single LLM, while TF--IDF with logistic regression was stronger on same-source data but declined across sources. The largest recorded benchmark-disagreement group contained cases in which a reference diagnosis appeared in the Top-3 and passed criterion checking but was not selected as the final primary diagnosis. Among cases available for criterion analysis, this group accounted for 29.7\% internally and 25.6\% on MDD-5k. This pattern motivates the hypothesis of a primary-diagnosis selection bottleneck, but it does not establish a causal or clinically validated bottleneck. None of the tested primary-diagnosis selection methods clearly improved over Direct-Answer.

HiED does not establish superior final-diagnosis accuracy. Its main contribution is an auditable two-path architecture and stage-wise evaluation framework that makes candidate omission, criterion incompatibility, missing information, and primary-diagnosis selection disagreement separately inspectable for the same case. All results come from synthetic Chinese transcripts and do not constitute clinical validation.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Large language models, multi-agent systems, psychiatric differential diagnosis, diagnostic criteria, clinical decision support, auditable AI

\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:introduction}

\section{Background and Motivation}

Taiwan's National Health Insurance system allows patients to access specialists and large medical centers directly. During a first psychiatric visit, the clinician must collect the patient's history, assess current symptoms and risks, and form an initial diagnosis from limited information. Important facts may still be missing, such as when the symptoms began, how long they have lasted, how they have changed, and whether another cause may explain them~\citep{lin2020outpatient}.

Patients may also describe emotional distress through everyday or physical complaints. In Chinese-speaking settings, they may first report poor sleep, fatigue, dizziness, chest tightness, or stomach problems instead of saying that they feel depressed or anxious~\citep{kleinman1982neurasthenia,ryder2008somatic}. The clinician must therefore turn a free-form conversation into clinical evidence while keeping the full context of the interview in view.

This task is difficult because several mental disorders share the same symptoms. Depression, anxiety, stress-related disorders, obsessive-compulsive disorder, somatic symptom disorders, and sleep disorders may all involve poor sleep, fatigue, poor concentration, and reduced daily function. A diagnosis cannot be made from one symptom alone. Each disorder has its own rules for key symptoms, symptom count, duration, effects on daily life, and the exclusion of other causes~\citep{who2019icd10}.

Even after these rules are checked, more than one diagnosis may still fit the same transcript. The symptoms may come from one main disorder, or more than one disorder may be present at the same time. The second case is called comorbidity~\citep{brown2001comorbidity}. In other cases, the transcript may not contain enough information to support one clear primary diagnosis. Structured diagnostic studies also report limited agreement for some common disorders~\citep{regier2013dsm5}.

A first psychiatric visit is therefore a comparison among possible diagnoses under incomplete evidence. It involves three linked decisions: forming candidate diagnoses, checking diagnostic criteria, and selecting a primary diagnosis while considering possible comorbidity. A mistake or uncertainty can appear at any of these stages. Time pressure may also increase the risk of focusing too early on one diagnosis~\citep{croskerry2003cognitive}.

For this reason, a useful decision-support system should provide more information than one final diagnosis. It should show the ranked candidate diagnoses, the state of each diagnostic criterion, and the information that is still missing. These outputs can help clinicians review the case and decide what should be checked next.

\section{Problem Definition and Challenges}
\label{sec:problem-definition}

Large language models can process clinical dialogue, and medical
multi-agent systems can divide diagnostic tasks across several model
calls~\citep{li2024taiwan,raballo2025,sarma2025,tang2024medagents,kim2024mdagents}.
However, a final diagnosis alone does not show how the system reached
that result or where a benchmark disagreement became visible.

The first challenge is incomplete evidence. This thesis studies
differential diagnosis from one fixed psychiatric interview transcript.
The system cannot ask follow-up questions or use information that is
absent from the transcript. Important distinctions, such as symptom
onset, duration, episode history, symptom order, and exclusionary causes,
may therefore remain unresolved.

The second challenge is comparison among compatible diagnoses. Several
disorders may pass their own rules for the same transcript. The system
must still decide which diagnosis best explains the current presentation,
which diagnoses should remain differential alternatives, and whether
another diagnosis should be recorded as comorbid.

The third challenge is evaluation. A final accuracy score does not show
whether a reference diagnosis was omitted from the candidate list, did
not enter the criterion-compatible set, or remained available but was
not selected as the final primary diagnosis. These patterns require
different improvements.

The main problem addressed in this thesis is therefore how to keep
candidate formation, criterion checking, and primary-diagnosis selection
separately visible for the same case. The resulting analysis describes
agreement with dataset reference labels and does not determine the
clinically correct diagnosis.

\section{Overview of HiED and Study Scope}

This thesis proposes HiED, a hybrid, evidence-grounded multi-agent framework for Chinese psychiatric interview transcripts. HiED separates the diagnostic process into two paths. The diagnosis path produces a ranked differential diagnosis and a proposed primary diagnosis. The criterion-checking path records whether the transcript supports, does not support, or lacks enough information for each configured diagnostic criterion.

HiED keeps the ranked candidates, criterion states, criterion-compatible diagnoses, and final primary diagnosis as separate outputs. This allows candidate generation, criterion checking, and primary diagnosis selection to be evaluated separately. A direct Single LLM baseline reads the same transcript but mainly returns the final diagnostic output.

In this thesis, \emph{auditable} means that these outputs are saved and can be reviewed after the system finishes. It does not mean that the outputs are clinically correct. The completed experiments use synthetic Chinese dialogue datasets and contain no clinician-derived evaluation. A proposed single-psychiatrist review of selected LingxiDiag cases is described as future work. Real-patient evaluation is outside the scope of this thesis.

\section{Contributions}

This thesis makes two main contributions.

First, it proposes a two-path hybrid framework for psychiatric differential diagnosis. The diagnosis path produces ranked candidate diagnoses, while the criterion-checking path records criterion-level support and forms a criterion-compatible set. HiED keeps these outputs together with the final primary diagnosis so that they can be reviewed separately.

Second, it introduces a stage-wise evaluation method for locating benchmark disagreement. The method separately measures whether a reference diagnosis appears in the Top-3, enters the criterion-compatible set, and is selected as the final primary diagnosis. This analysis is applied to two synthetic Chinese dialogue datasets. The results describe benchmark agreement under the tested settings and do not establish the clinically correct diagnosis.

\chapter{Related Work}
\label{ch:related}

This chapter reviews five groups of earlier work that are closely related to this thesis: psychiatric LLM diagnosis, retrieval with similar examples, medical multi-agent systems, criterion-level records that can be checked, and primary-diagnosis selection. The goal is not to rank the systems. The goal is to show what part of diagnosis each system studies, what outputs it keeps, and what questions are still open.

\section{Psychiatric LLM Diagnosis}

Psychiatric LLM studies cover many different tasks. Some test psychiatric knowledge or licensing questions. Others use short clinical cases, focus on one disorder, detect depression from text, diagnose from a fixed record, or support an interactive consultation~\citep{qiu2023largeai,li2024taiwan,raballo2025,sarma2025,li2025addreview,mcduff2025amie}. These tasks are not the same. They differ in the evidence given to the model, the number of possible diagnoses, the required output, and the reference used for scoring.

Screening is also different from choosing among several similar diagnoses. A screening system may only need to find a broad depression or anxiety signal. A differential-diagnosis system must compare disorders that share symptoms and still choose one primary diagnosis. This choice may depend on symptom duration, which symptoms started first, illness course, other possible causes, and comorbidity. LingxiDiagBench shows this difference: performance falls as the task moves from broad depression--anxiety classification to comorbidity recognition and twelve-category diagnosis~\citep{xu2026lingxidiagbench}. MDD-5k is another synthetic Chinese dialogue resource generated from anonymized patient cases~\citep{yin2024mdd5k}. Both datasets support repeatable experiments, but neither replaces testing on natural clinical conversations.

Another key difference is whether the system can ask more questions. MIND, WiseMind/ProAI, MAGI, DSM5AgentFlow, and conversational diagnostic AI study interactive workflows that can ask questions during the assessment~\citep{li2026mind,wu2026wisemind,bi2025magi,ozgun2025dsm5agentflow,tu2025conversational}. MentalSeek-Dx and MoodAngels instead work with information that is already present in a record or transcript~\citep{sun2026mentalseek,xiao2025moodangels}. These settings test different abilities. An interactive system can collect new evidence. A fixed-input system must work with the same possibly incomplete record. This thesis uses the fixed-input setting, so the model receives no credit for asking follow-up questions.

Most published studies still focus on the final diagnosis. A final score does not show whether a reference diagnosis was never proposed, failed a criterion rule, or remained available but was not selected as primary. This gap motivates a stage-wise evaluation instead of final-label accuracy alone.

\section{Retrieval with Similar Cases}

Retrieval can have two different roles. Knowledge retrieval adds documents, guidelines, or other factual sources~\citep{lewis2020rag}. Example retrieval selects labeled examples or similar cases and places them in the prompt for in-context learning~\citep{liu2022incontext}. This thesis uses example retrieval. The retrieved cases come from the training source. They are examples for the model, not medical evidence about the current patient.

Similar examples can show common wording, the expected output format, possible labels, and common symptom--diagnosis patterns. However, in-context learning depends strongly on which examples are selected~\citep{liu2022incontext}. A retrieved case describes another patient or synthetic case. It may guide the model, but it does not show that the current patient has the same diagnosis.

Retrieval can also hurt performance. The examples reflect the wording and label balance of the source corpus. Frequent classes may appear many times. Very similar wording may encourage shortcuts, while a weakly related example may distract the model. Retrieval should therefore be tested as one complete setting rather than assumed to improve diagnosis.

A label-aware policy may show a wider range of diagnoses and reduce repeated examples from frequent classes. However, balancing also changes the number, order, similarity, total length, and mix of examples. A comparison between global similarity retrieval and a balanced policy is therefore a comparison between two complete retrieval settings. It is not a clean test of label balance alone.

Earlier work shows that example choice matters for in-context learning, but it does not show which retrieval policy is best for fine-grained psychiatric diagnosis. Retrieval policies should therefore be compared within the same architecture and scoring contract.

\section{Medical Multi-Agent Systems}

A medical multi-agent system uses several model calls and gives them different roles. One call may propose a diagnosis, another may extract evidence, another may criticize an answer, and another may make the final decision. Other systems use consultation, debate, voting, or consensus. MedAgents uses several role-based medical experts, while MDAgents changes the collaboration plan according to estimated task difficulty~\citep{tang2024medagents,kim2024mdagents}. Multi-agent debate is a related method in which agents exchange and revise competing answers~\citep{du2024debate}.

These designs may help. A narrow role can focus on one task. A second model call may find missing evidence. A checker may compare a diagnosis with criteria, and a structured workflow can save intermediate outputs for review. These are possible benefits, not guarantees. More agents or more discussion rounds do not automatically lead to better diagnostic accuracy.

Multi-agent systems can also repeat the same mistake. Agents that use the same model family, similar prompts, and the same evidence may fail in similar ways. A wrong candidate or an unsupported evidence statement can pass into later steps. Free-form discussion may change several parts of the workflow at once, and the last component may still choose the wrong answer even when a useful alternative was mentioned earlier.

This makes the source of a performance change hard to identify. If the candidate generator, evidence record, discussion process, and final rule all change together, a higher or lower final score is a result of the whole system. It does not show which part caused the change. A comparison with a Single LLM may also change the prompt, context, number of model calls, and compute.

For this reason, candidate generation, criterion checking, and final-primary selection should be saved and evaluated separately. This does not make the components independent, but it shows more clearly where the recorded disagreement appears.

\section{Auditability and Criterion-Level Records}
\label{sec:auditability-definition}

An explanation that sounds clear may still fail to match the factors that led to the answer~\citep{turpin2023unfaithful}. This thesis uses four related terms. \emph{Explainability} means the account shown to a person. \emph{Interpretability} means how easy the decision process is to understand. \emph{Faithfulness} asks whether the account matches the factors that produced the output. \emph{Auditability} means that an external record is saved and can be checked or replayed. These properties are different and should be evaluated separately in healthcare~\citep{rudin2019stop,huang2024xiai}.

Diagnostic-chain and symptom-based methods can show intermediate diagnoses, confidence values, or clinically motivated features~\citep{chen2025cod,lee2026interpretable,lyu2026depllm}. Psychiatric systems may also connect outputs to guidelines, structured interviews, symptom-to-criterion links, and supporting or conflicting evidence~\citep{li2026mind,bi2025magi,ozgun2025dsm5agentflow}. Constraint-logic methods make the rule layer easier to inspect by turning diagnostic criteria into explicit programs~\citep{kim2025clp}.

These records are useful only when the criteria and evidence links are reasonable. A model may write an unsupported evidence note. A criterion may be represented poorly, and a visible rule may still contain a weak clinical assumption. Expert review of model-generated diagnostic rules is still needed~\citep{kim2025clp}. Criterion-level outputs are therefore model records that can be reviewed. They are not clinician-validated evidence or clinical truth.

An auditable system should keep the source evidence, criterion states, rule results, intermediate diagnoses, and final decision. Even then, the record does not reveal hidden model reasoning. A criterion layer may be used only for review, or it may also affect final selection. These two roles should be tested separately because good evidence recording or high compatibility inclusion does not guarantee a good primary diagnosis.

The remaining gap is therefore not only whether a system keeps intermediate records. The records must also be evaluated together on the same cases: Was the diagnosis available as a candidate? Did it enter the compatible set? Was it selected as the final primary diagnosis?

\section{Primary Diagnosis Selection}

Criterion compatibility and primary-diagnosis selection answer different questions. Compatibility asks whether one diagnosis fits the available transcript under a defined rule. Because psychiatric disorders share symptoms, more than one diagnosis may pass its own rule. Compatibility alone does not show whether a diagnosis should be primary, comorbid, secondary, or only a differential alternative.

Structured medical knowledge can help organize evidence. MedKGI combines a medical knowledge graph, guided questions, and a structured dialogue state to seek useful information during diagnosis~\citep{wang2025medkgi}. DKEC builds knowledge graphs from outside medical sources and uses their links for multilabel diagnosis prediction~\citep{ge2024dkec}. These studies show how structured information can help collect evidence or predict labels. Their tested goals are different from choosing one primary diagnosis among several criterion-compatible psychiatric candidates in a fixed transcript.

A primary selector may need to compare the candidates directly. Useful information may include symptom timing, duration, illness course, past episodes, other possible causes, which syndrome explains the case best, and whether more than one disorder is present. The reviewed methods can organize evidence or find plausible labels, but they do not directly test the case in which a benchmark-reference diagnosis remains ranked and criterion-compatible while another diagnosis is selected as the final primary diagnosis.

This gap motivates three separate measures: Top-3 candidate coverage, reference-diagnosis inclusion in the parent-level criterion-compatible set, and final-primary agreement.

\FloatBarrier

The reviewed work leaves five questions that are directly related to this thesis:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Conventional classifiers, Single LLMs, and multi-stage systems often use different prompts, model calls, retrieval settings, and output contracts. A final score therefore compares complete systems, not one isolated component.
    \item Final-label accuracy does not show whether a reference diagnosis was missing from the candidates, absent from the criterion-compatible set, or available in both views but not selected as the final primary diagnosis.
    \item Example retrieval depends on which examples are selected. Global similarity retrieval and label-aware balancing should therefore be compared within each architecture.
    \item Multi-agent and criterion-based selection methods often change several parts of the workflow at once. This makes it hard to claim that one final-selection component caused the result.
    \item A stage-wise analysis should be checked on a second synthetic source. One corpus cannot show whether the same output views remain useful after the data source changes.
\end{enumerate}

HiED addresses these questions by saving the ranked candidates, criterion states, criterion-compatible set, and final primary diagnosis under clear comparison rules.

This thesis uses the following research questions:

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=1.5cm,labelwidth=1.1cm,itemsep=0pt,parsep=0pt,topsep=0pt,partopsep=0pt]
\item[RQ1] How does HiED perform relative to conventional classification and a Single LLM on the same internal split and parent-label evaluation?
\item[RQ2] Where is benchmark disagreement recorded across candidate generation, criterion checking, compatibility analysis, and final-primary selection?
\item[RQ3] How do similar-case retrieval strategies affect Single and HiED?
\item[RQ4] Do the tested primary-selection strategies reduce the recorded selection disagreement?
\item[RQ5] Does the stage-wise analysis remain useful under a second synthetic source?
\end{description}

RQ3 first compares retrieval settings on the public-validation split and freezes the selected settings. RQ1 then tests those settings on the fixed internal held-out split. RQ2 uses the internal HiED trace to locate recorded disagreement. RQ4 compares the tested primary-selection methods. RQ5 applies the same output views to MDD-5k and uses the cross-corpus results to define the synthetic-source boundary.

\section{System Overview}
\label{sec:compared-architectures}

\subsection{HiED Two-Path Architecture}
\label{sec:hied-two-path-architecture}

HiED separates the diagnostic process into two connected paths, as shown
in Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline-thesis-rewrite}.

The upper path is the \emph{diagnosis path}. It uses the transcript and,
when enabled, similar cases to produce the Top-3, a proposed primary
diagnosis, and an optional comorbid diagnosis.

The lower path is the \emph{criterion-checking path}. It compares the
same transcript with the configured criteria for all fourteen diagnostic
categories. It records the state of each criterion and forms a
criterion-compatible set.

The finalization step receives the outputs from both paths and records
the final primary diagnosis.

\subsection{Single LLM Baseline}
\label{sec:single-architecture}

For comparison in this thesis, the Single LLM can be understood as the
direct diagnosis path without the separate criterion-checking path. It
reads the same transcript and may receive the same type of similar cases,
but its main output is the final diagnostic result.

The Single LLM does not provide the Top-3, criterion states, or
criterion-compatible set as separate outputs for stage-wise analysis.
This does not mean that the model considered no alternatives. It means
that those intermediate results are not available in the same recorded
form as HiED.

\subsection{Recorded Output Difference}
\label{sec:recorded-output-differences}

Single and HiED both produce a final diagnosis. HiED also keeps the
Top-3 and criterion results so that candidate formation, criterion
checking, and final selection can be examined separately.

This comparison does not isolate the effect of using more agents. Single
and HiED differ in both workflow and output. Their final performance
therefore compares two complete systems.

\section{Diagnosis Path}
\label{sec:retrieval}
\label{sec:diagnosis-path}

The diagnosis path forms a differential diagnosis from the transcript.
It contains optional similar-case retrieval and a Diagnostician.

\subsection{Similar-Case Retrieval}

Similar-case retrieval selects labeled examples from the training data
and provides them to the Diagnostician. These cases may show similar
language and possible diagnostic labels. They are examples for the
model; they are not evidence about the current patient.

The study compares three retrieval settings: no retrieval, Global Top-5
retrieval, and parent-balanced retrieval.

\paragraph{Global Top-5 retrieval.}

This method selects the five cases with the highest similarity scores.
Because the dataset is imbalanced, the selected cases may come mainly
from one frequent diagnosis.

\paragraph{Parent-balanced retrieval.}

This method allows at most one case from each evaluation parent label.
Its purpose is to show the Diagnostician a wider range of possible
diagnoses.

Retrieved cases are used only by the diagnosis path. The Criterion
Checkers do not receive them. Retrieval can therefore change the Top-3
and the proposed primary diagnosis, but it does not directly change the
criterion-checking input.

\subsection{Diagnostician Outputs}
\label{sec:diagnostician-outputs}

The Diagnostician reads the transcript and any retrieved cases. It
produces three types of output:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item a ranked differential diagnosis;
    \item one proposed primary diagnosis; and
    \item at most one optional comorbid diagnosis.
\end{enumerate}

This thesis uses the first three distinct evaluation labels in the ranked
list as the \emph{Top-3}. The Top-3 shows which diagnoses remain under
consideration. A diagnosis may therefore appear in the Top-3 even when
another diagnosis is proposed as primary.

The optional comorbid diagnosis has a different meaning from a candidate
diagnosis. A candidate is an alternative that may explain the case. A
comorbid diagnosis is proposed as an additional disorder that may be
present together with the primary diagnosis.

The ranked list, proposed primary diagnosis, and comorbid diagnosis come
from the same Diagnostician. They are different output roles, not
independent expert opinions.

\section{Criterion-Checking Path}
\label{sec:criterion-checking-path}

The criterion-checking path examines the transcript at the diagnostic
criterion level. It records which criteria are supported, which are not
supported, and which cannot be decided from the available information.

\subsection{Criterion Checkers}
\label{sec:criterion-checkers}

Each of the fourteen configured diagnoses has one Criterion Checker.
Every checker receives the same transcript and the criteria for its
assigned diagnosis.

All fourteen diagnoses are checked for every case. The criterion-checking
path is not limited to the Diagnostician's Top-3. A diagnosis may
therefore pass the criterion rules even when it is absent from the
Top-3.

Each criterion receives one of three states, shown in
Table~\ref{tab:criterion-state-definitions}.

\subsection{Compatibility Auditor}
\label{sec:compatibility-auditor}

After criterion checking, the Compatibility Auditor applies the
study-specific rule for each diagnosis. A rule may require core symptoms,
a minimum number of supported criteria, a required duration, or a
combination of these conditions.

The auditor uses the criterion states and does not reread the transcript.
Diagnoses that pass their rules are placed in the
\emph{criterion-compatible set}. The complete rules are listed in
Appendix~\ref{app:configured-profile}.

\subsection{Meaning of Criterion Compatibility}
\label{sec:criterion-compatibility-boundary}

A diagnosis in the criterion-compatible set has passed the study rule
using the information in the transcript. This result does not mean that
the diagnosis is clinically correct.

Several diagnoses may pass their rules for the same transcript because
mental disorders share symptoms and important information may be
missing. Criterion compatibility also does not decide which diagnosis
should be primary. It answers whether one diagnosis is supported under
its own rule, not whether it is better than the other candidates.

\section{Primary Diagnosis Selection}
\label{sec:primary-selection}

The diagnosis path provides the Top-3 and a proposed primary diagnosis.
The criterion-checking path provides criterion states and a
criterion-compatible set. The finalization step uses these outputs to
record one final primary diagnosis.

\subsection{Output Roles}
\label{sec:diagnostic-output-roles}

A candidate diagnosis is an alternative under consideration. A proposed
primary diagnosis is the Diagnostician's first choice. A comorbid
diagnosis is an additional disorder that may be present at the same time.
A criterion-compatible diagnosis is one that passes its own study rule.
These roles are kept separate because none of them alone determines the
final primary diagnosis.

\subsection{Direct-Answer}
\label{sec:direct-answer-policy}

Direct-Answer (DA) keeps the primary diagnosis proposed by the
Diagnostician. It may also keep one optional comorbid diagnosis. The
criterion results remain available for review but do not replace the
Diagnostician's choice.

\subsection{Nominate-then-Select}
\label{sec:nominate-then-select-policy}

Nominate-then-Select (NtS) combines the Top-3 with the
criterion-compatible set. It checks the candidates in ranked order and
selects the highest-ranked diagnosis that also passes the compatibility
rule. NtS returns one reselected primary diagnosis.

\subsection{Relationship between DA and NtS}
\label{sec:da-nts-relationship}
\label{sec:primary-selection-boundary}

DA and NtS use the same transcript, Top-3, proposed primary diagnosis,
criterion states, and compatible set. They differ only in how these
outputs are used for the final selection.

This makes the comparison useful for studying primary diagnosis
selection. However, a disagreement with the dataset label is still a
benchmark disagreement, not a confirmed clinical error. The transcript
may be incomplete, and the dataset may not provide a unique
psychiatrist-reviewed primary diagnosis.

\section{Chapter Summary}

This chapter described how Single and HiED process one fixed psychiatric
interview transcript. Single mainly provides the final diagnostic
output. HiED also keeps the Top-3, criterion states, and
criterion-compatible set as separate outputs.

HiED has two connected paths. The diagnosis path forms the Top-3 and
proposes a primary diagnosis. The criterion-checking path records
criterion states for all configured diagnoses and forms the compatible
set. DA and NtS then use the same earlier outputs but apply different
rules to select the final primary diagnosis.

These outputs make candidate formation, criterion checking, and primary
diagnosis selection separately visible. They support later stage-wise
analysis, but they remain model-generated records and do not establish
clinical correctness.

\chapter{Datasets, Label Projection, and Evaluation Protocol}
\label{ch:data}

\section{Configured Output Space and Gold-Label Projection}
\label{sec:gold-label-conventions}

Throughout this thesis, the dataset-provided diagnoses used for scoring are referred to as \emph{benchmark gold labels}. The term \emph{gold} denotes the official benchmark reference and does not imply a uniquely correct clinical diagnosis.

The main system uses fourteen configured ICD-10 diagnostic categories, listed in Table~\ref{tab:configured-profile-summary}. For evaluation, subtype-level categories are projected to eleven broader parent labels. Let $G_{\mathrm{raw}}(x)$ be the raw benchmark gold codes for case $x$, and let $\pi$ map subtype-level codes to their scoring parents, mapping F41.0, F41.1, and F41.2 to F41 and F43.1 and F43.2 to F43. Codes already defined at the scoring-parent level are left unchanged. Let $\mathcal{C}$ be the set of eleven configured scoring parents. The in-scope gold-label set is
\[
G_{\mathrm{in}}(x)=\{\pi(c):c\in G_{\mathrm{raw}}(x),\ \pi(c)\in\mathcal{C}\}.
\]
The scored gold-label set is then
\[
G(x)=
\begin{cases}
G_{\mathrm{in}}(x), & G_{\mathrm{in}}(x)\neq\varnothing,\\
\{\mathrm{Others}\}, & G_{\mathrm{in}}(x)=\varnothing.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus, when a case contains both an out-of-scope code and F41.1, its scored gold-label set is $\{\mathrm{F41}\}$ rather than $\{\mathrm{Others},\mathrm{F41}\}$. A case is in scope when $G_{\mathrm{in}}(x)$ is nonempty and out of scope otherwise. Because the main system has neither an \emph{Others} output nor a trained unknown detector, it cannot correctly represent an out-of-scope case. TF--IDF with logistic regression is trained with \emph{Others} as a scoring class, whereas the Majority, Single, and HiED systems cannot emit \emph{Others}. The full 1,000-case comparison therefore combines in-scope diagnosis prediction with out-of-scope handling; it is not a common eleven-class in-scope comparison.

For case $x$, $G(x)$ is the deduplicated gold-label set after parent-level projection; we refer to the individual labels in $G(x)$ as \emph{benchmark gold parents}. LingxiDiag cases may contain more than one gold parent label, and neither dataset provides a psychiatrist-reviewed unique clinical primary diagnosis. The first-listed gold parent is used only for explicitly single-label descriptive analyses such as the confusion matrix.

\section{Datasets, Case Construction, and Split Roles}

The study uses two synthetic Chinese psychiatric dialogue datasets. LingxiDiag-16K, the dataset underlying LingxiDiagBench, is used for system development and internal held-out evaluation, whereas MDD-5k is used for external cross-dataset evaluation.

\subsection{LingxiDiag-16K}

LingxiDiag-16K is the synthetic Chinese psychiatric dialogue dataset underlying LingxiDiagBench and was generated for static diagnostic classification and interactive consultation tasks~\citep{xu2026lingxidiagbench}. It provides a fixed diagnostic label space and sufficient cases for training, configuration selection, and internal held-out evaluation. The published LingxiDiagBench results also show that fine-grained disorder-level diagnosis is substantially more difficult than broad depression--anxiety classification.

The accessible release used in this study contained 14,000 training-source cases and 1,000 public-validation cases. The official test file was not available in the accessible release used when the experiments were designed.

The 1,000-case internal held-out set was sampled from the 14,000-case training source using the first-listed projected parent label to approximately match the public-validation class distribution. A fixed random seed was used for reproducibility. The remaining 13,000 cases were used to train conventional baselines and build the retrieval index. The first-listed label served only as a stratification variable and was not treated as a clinically validated primary diagnosis. Case-ID checks confirmed that held-out cases were excluded from baseline training, the retrieval index, and all configuration-selection procedures. No additional semantic near-duplicate screening was performed.

\section{Prediction Views and Metrics}
\label{sec:prediction-metrics}

The evaluator keeps four output views separate:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item The \emph{primary view}, $p_1(x)$, is the final primary diagnosis.
\item The \emph{ranked view}, $R_3(x)$, contains the first three distinct parent labels in a method's ranked output. TF--IDF with logistic regression obtains this view from ranked class scores. HiED obtains it from the Diagnostician ranking before DA or NtS finalization. A method that does not produce a ranked diagnostic list has no $R_3(x)$ view.
\item The \emph{compatibility view} has two levels. $L_{\mathrm{cat}}(x)$ is the set of the fourteen configured ICD-10 categories that pass their study-specific rules. The parent-level set is $L_{\mathrm{par}}(x)=\{\pi(c):c\in L_{\mathrm{cat}}(x)\}$ after subtype projection and deduplication. NtS uses $L_{\mathrm{cat}}(x)$ to compare configured category codes, while benchmark inclusion and $I(x)$ use $L_{\mathrm{par}}(x)$.
\item The \emph{multilabel view}, $M(x)$, is the complete set of emitted parent labels used for Exact Match and F1. For DA, it contains the final primary diagnosis and any emitted comorbid parent label. For a single-label method, it contains one label.
\end{enumerate}

Top-3 Accuracy is reported only for methods that produce a ranked diagnostic list. TF--IDF with logistic regression forms this list from its class scores, while HiED uses the Diagnostician ranking. Single does not provide the same ranked differential diagnosis, so no Top-3 Accuracy is reported for Single. The Majority baseline also has no Top-3 value. Their additional or single-label outputs remain part of the complete diagnosis set used for Exact Match and F1 where applicable.

Because a case may contain more than one gold label, Top-1 and Top-3 each count a case as correct when at least one projected benchmark gold label appears in the evaluated view. Exact Match, also called subset accuracy, instead requires the complete predicted label set to equal the complete gold-label set. These are benchmark-agreement measures, not independent measures of the patient's true clinical diagnosis.

For example, suppose that the projected gold-label set contains F41, while a ranked output places F32 first and F41.1 second. Because F41.1 is projected to its scoring parent F41, the case is incorrect under Top-1 evaluation but correct under Top-3 evaluation.

DA may emit a final primary diagnosis and one optional comorbid diagnosis, whereas NtS emits one reselected primary diagnosis. Their Top-1 comparison therefore evaluates the primary diagnosis directly, but differences in Exact Match, Macro-F1, or Weighted-F1 also reflect output cardinality and cannot be interpreted as isolated effects of primary re-selection.

Table~\ref{tab:metric-contract} summarizes which output view is evaluated by each measure. The subsequent equations give the case-level definitions used by the scoring implementation.

\hline

Top-1 and Top-3 Accuracy are computed over all evaluation cases. Top-3 Accuracy is reported only for a method with a ranked diagnostic list. Gold-label inclusion is computed with $L_{\mathrm{par}}(x)$ over cases with at least one non-\emph{Others} gold parent covered by a corresponding Criterion Checker. Category-level and parent-level compatible-set sizes are reported separately because subtype projection can collapse several configured categories into one scoring parent.

\[
\mathrm{Top\text{-}1\ Accuracy}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{x=1}^{N}\mathbb{1}[p_1(x)\in G(x)],
\]
\[
\mathrm{Top\text{-}3\ Accuracy}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{x=1}^{N}\mathbb{1}[R_3(x)\cap G(x)\neq\varnothing],
\]
\[
\mathrm{Exact\ Match}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{x=1}^{N}\mathbb{1}[M(x)=G(x)].
\]

Let the fixed scoring universe be
\[
\mathcal{Y}=\{\mathrm{F20},\mathrm{F31},\mathrm{F32},\mathrm{F39},\mathrm{F41},\mathrm{F42},\mathrm{F43},\mathrm{F45},\mathrm{F51},\mathrm{F98},\mathrm{Z71},\mathrm{Others}\}.
\]
For class $c\in\mathcal{Y}$, define $y_{x,c}=\mathbb{1}[c\in G(x)]$ and $\hat{y}_{x,c}=\mathbb{1}[c\in M(x)]$. The one-vs-rest counts are
\[
\mathrm{TP}_c=\sum_x y_{x,c}\hat{y}_{x,c},\qquad
\mathrm{FP}_c=\sum_x (1-y_{x,c})\hat{y}_{x,c},\qquad
\mathrm{FN}_c=\sum_x y_{x,c}(1-\hat{y}_{x,c}).
\]
The class-level F1 score is
\[
\mathrm{F1}_c=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{2\mathrm{TP}_c}{2\mathrm{TP}_c+\mathrm{FP}_c+\mathrm{FN}_c}, & 2\mathrm{TP}_c+\mathrm{FP}_c+\mathrm{FN}_c>0,\\[6pt]
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
This zero case matches the scoring implementation's \texttt{zero\_division=0} setting: a class with no positive support and no positive prediction contributes zero rather than being removed from the fixed class universe. Macro-F1 and Weighted-F1 are then
\[
\mathrm{Macro\text{-}F1}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{Y}|}\sum_{c\in\mathcal{Y}}\mathrm{F1}_c,
\]
\[
\mathrm{Weighted\text{-}F1}=\frac{\sum_{c\in\mathcal{Y}} n_c\,\mathrm{F1}_c}{\sum_{c\in\mathcal{Y}} n_c},
\qquad n_c=\sum_x y_{x,c}.
\]
Macro-F1 gives equal weight to all twelve scoring classes, including rare classes and \emph{Others}. Weighted-F1 gives more influence to classes with greater gold support and is therefore driven mainly by the frequent F32 and F41 labels in this study. Both measures evaluate the complete emitted set $M(x)$ and are sensitive to how many diagnoses a method emits.

The preserved HiED case-level outputs were independently rescored and
reproduced the reported metrics. Detailed recalculation results,
output-cardinality statistics, and result-provenance boundaries are
reported in Appendix~\ref{app:supporting}.

\section{Case-Level Indicators for Disagreement Localization}
\label{sec:case-level-indicators}

The measures defined in the preceding section summarize performance across evaluation cases. For case-level disagreement localization, this thesis uses three corresponding binary indicators: Top-3 candidate detection, reference-diagnosis inclusion in the parent-level compatible set, and final-primary agreement.

For case $x$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_3(x) &= \mathbb{1}[R_3(x)\cap G(x)\neq\varnothing],\\
I(x) &= \mathbb{1}[L_{\mathrm{par}}(x)\cap G(x)\neq\varnothing],\\
S(x) &= \mathbb{1}[p_1(x)\in G(x)].
\end{aligned}
\]

$D_3(x)$ is the case-level indicator underlying Top-3 Accuracy: it equals one when at least one benchmark gold label appears in the ranked Top-3. $I(x)$ equals one when the parent-level compatible set contains at least one benchmark gold label. $S(x)$ is the case-level indicator underlying Top-1 Accuracy: it equals one when the final primary diagnosis matches at least one benchmark gold label.

Accordingly, Top-3 Accuracy is the mean of $D_3(x)$ across all evaluation cases, and Top-1 Accuracy is the mean of $S(x)$. The reference-diagnosis inclusion rate is the mean of $I(x)$ over checker-eligible cases.

$D_3$ and $S$ are available for all 1,000 internal evaluation cases, whereas $I$ is defined only for the 915 checker-eligible cases whose benchmark gold-label set contains at least one non-\emph{Others} parent represented by a corresponding Criterion Checker. The three aggregate rates therefore use different denominators and must not be interpreted as consecutive stages of one numerical funnel.

These indicators describe complementary recorded output views for the same case. They do not imply that every finalization policy executes candidate ranking, criterion checking, and primary selection in this chronological order. In the preserved 1,000-case internal output, every benchmark-consistent final primary diagnosis also appeared in the recorded Top-3; no $D_3=0,S=1$ case occurred. The $D_3=1,I=1,S=0$ profile is therefore a final-primary-output disagreement profile. It motivates the hypothesis of a primary-diagnosis selection bottleneck, but it does not prove a causal or clinically validated bottleneck.

\section{Statistical Analysis}

The case is the unit of analysis, and all statistical tests are two-sided with a significance level of $\alpha=0.05$. When confidence intervals are reported for a single binary proportion, 95\% Wilson intervals are used. Paired comparisons require identical case IDs and evaluation-metric definitions. For binary correctness outcomes, the effect estimate is the paired difference in correctness proportions, and the exact McNemar test is computed from the two discordant counts: cases correct only under the first configuration and cases correct only under the second. Main paired comparisons report the effect estimate and its confidence interval; detailed discordant counts and $p$-values are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:supporting}.

Confidence intervals for paired accuracy differences and finalization difference-in-differences contrasts are estimated by resampling case IDs with replacement using 10,000 paired bootstrap replicates and seed 42. The 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles define the reported 95\% confidence interval. Reported $p$-values are interpreted together with effect direction, confidence intervals, and discordant counts rather than as standalone evidence.

Holm correction is applied when multiple prespecified tests belong to the same comparison family defined in Chapter~\ref{ch:experimental}; unrelated or isolated comparisons are not pooled into an artificial family. A non-significant result indicates insufficient evidence of a difference and is not interpreted as evidence of equivalence.

\section{Retrieval Selection and Internal Comparison}
\label{sec:benchmark-retrieval-design}

\subsection{Retrieval Settings}

Retrieval is tested separately for Single and HiED under three settings:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item no retrieval;
    \item Global Top-5 retrieval; and
    \item parent-balanced retrieval.
\end{enumerate}

Global Top-5 selects the five most similar training cases.
Parent-balanced retrieval limits repeated cases from the same evaluation
parent label and provides a wider range of diagnostic examples.

Retrieved cases are given only to the diagnosis path. The Criterion
Checkers receive the current transcript and the configured diagnostic
criteria, but they do not receive the retrieved cases.

The two retrieval methods differ in both similarity and case
composition. Their comparison therefore evaluates the complete retrieval
settings rather than the effect of label balance alone.

\subsection{Validation Selection (RQ3)}

Single and HiED select their retrieval settings separately because they
produce different outputs.

For Single, Global Top-5 was retained because it had the highest
validation Top-1 Accuracy (0.519). For HiED--DA, parent-balanced
retrieval was retained because it had the highest validation Top-3
Accuracy (0.800), Macro-F1 (0.181), and Weighted-F1 (0.440), although
Global Top-5 had a slightly higher Top-1 point estimate (0.525 versus
0.523). These choices were fixed before the held-out set was evaluated.

After these settings are selected, they are fixed before the internal
held-out set is evaluated. RQ3 compares the three retrieval settings
within each architecture. It does not use held-out performance to choose
a new setting.

\subsection{Internal System Comparison (RQ1)}

The main internal comparison uses the same fixed 1,000-case LingxiDiag
held-out set. It includes:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item the Majority baseline;
    \item TF--IDF with logistic regression;
    \item the validation-selected Single LLM; and
    \item the validation-selected HiED--DA system.
\end{enumerate}

All methods use the label mapping and evaluation measures defined in
Chapter~\ref{ch:data}. Top-1 Accuracy is the main common measure because
all methods produce one primary diagnosis.

Top-3 Accuracy is reported only for methods that produce a ranked
diagnostic list. TF--IDF with logistic regression obtains this ranking
from its class scores, while HiED obtains it from the Diagnostician.
Single does not produce the same ranked differential diagnosis, so its
optional additional labels are not treated as HiED-style Top-3
candidates.

Exact Match, Macro-F1, and Weighted-F1 evaluate the complete diagnosis
set produced by each method. Because the systems differ in training,
retrieval, workflow, and outputs, this comparison evaluates complete
systems. It does not isolate the effect of using multiple agents.

DA and NtS use the same transcript, Top-3, proposed primary diagnosis,

rule used to select the final primary diagnosis.

whereas NtS returns one reselected primary diagnosis.

\subsection{Other Selection Methods}

Deterministic fusion reuses the recorded diagnosis and criterion results
but applies a different fixed rule.

Pairwise comparison and two-view debate add new LLM calls. Their results
therefore compare complete methods and cannot be attributed only to the
last selection step.

Self-consistency and confidence-informed self-consistency repeat the
Diagnostician inference. They test whether repeated generation and voting
reduce the instability of one diagnostic output.

Top-1 Accuracy is the common outcome for all selection methods. A paired
confidence interval or McNemar test is reported only when both methods
cover the same cases and retain the required case-level results. Other
comparisons are described using their aggregate results.

\section{External Evaluation and Supporting Analyses}
\label{sec:sensitivity-transfer-design}

RQ5 tests whether the same stage-wise analysis can be applied to MDD-5k,

The main external evaluation uses all 925 MDD-5k cases for Top-1 and

The thesis also includes three supporting analyses:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Model size.}
    Qwen3-4B, 8B, 14B, and 32B are compared on the same internal
    held-out set.

    \item \textbf{Cross-dataset TF--IDF transfer.}
    The TF--IDF classifier is trained on one synthetic dataset and tested

    \item \textbf{Common disagreement directions.}
    A first-listed-label confusion matrix and frequent
    reference-to-prediction pairs are used as descriptive analyses.
\end{enumerate}

The model-size and TF--IDF analyses help explain the limits of the
observed results. They do not provide evidence of performance on natural
clinical conversations.

The internal results support four main findings.

\section{Chapter Summary}

The external results answer RQ5 with a limited conclusion. The same
stage-wise analysis can be applied to MDD-5k, and the largest external
disagreement group has the same form as the largest internal group: a
reference diagnosis is already present in both the Top-3 and the
parent-level compatible set but is not selected as the final primary
diagnosis. This is an observed output profile rather than proof of a
causal selection bottleneck.

The numerical rates are not identical. In particular, reference-diagnosis
inclusion in the criterion-compatible set is lower on MDD-5k. The result
therefore supports the use of the same analysis framework, not stable
performance across datasets.

The external primary-diagnosis selection methods also do not provide a
clear improvement over DA. Finally, the TF--IDF transfer experiment shows
that its strong same-source performance falls when the evaluation dataset
changes.

These findings define a boundary across two synthetic Chinese dialogue
datasets. They do not establish performance on real psychiatric
consultations, superior generalization by HiED, or clinical validity.

\chapter{Detailed Analysis of Benchmark Disagreements}
\label{ch:error}

This chapter examines the internal benchmark disagreements identified
in Chapter~\ref{ch:results}. It focuses on the rank of the reference
diagnosis, frequent diagnosis directions, differences in the complete
diagnosis set, and patterns in the criterion states.

The analysis describes agreement with dataset reference diagnoses. It
does not identify clinically confirmed errors, reveal hidden model

\section{Analysis Scope}
\label{sec:ch8-stagewise-taxonomy}

Chapter~\ref{ch:results} divided the 915 checker-eligible cases into four
groups using three indicators:

\begin{itemize}
    \item $D_3$: whether a reference diagnosis appears in the Top-3;
    \item $I$: whether a reference diagnosis appears in the
    criterion-compatible set; and
    \item $S$: whether the final primary diagnosis matches a reference
    diagnosis.
\end{itemize}

This chapter focuses mainly on the 272 cases with
$D_3=1$, $I=1$, and $S=0$. In these cases, a reference diagnosis is

These cases are described as \emph{final-primary-output disagreements}.
They should not be treated as complete omissions of the reference
diagnosis. In some cases, a reference diagnosis may still appear as an

The analysis uses the observed case-level results from the 1,000-case
internal held-out set. The gold-informed upper-bound analysis reported
in Chapter~\ref{ch:results} is not an observed system group and is not
included here.

\section{Chapter Summary}
\label{sec:ch8-summary}

This chapter adds four findings to the stage-wise results reported in
Chapter~\ref{ch:results}.

First, in the 272-case final-primary-output disagreement group, the
reference diagnosis is more often ranked second than third. The system
therefore often keeps the reference diagnosis as a close alternative.

Second, the disagreement directions are concentrated. F41-to-F32 is the
largest direction and accounts for 58.1\% of the 272 cases. These raw
counts are affected by class frequency and should not be interpreted as
clinical misdiagnosis rates.

Third, a matching final primary diagnosis does not guarantee a matching
complete diagnosis set. Among the 518 cases with primary agreement, 109
still differ from the reference diagnosis set.

Fourth, the overall proportions of \texttt{met},
\texttt{not met}, and
\texttt{insufficient evidence} are similar between the
primary-agreement group and the largest primary-diagnosis disagreement
group. A simple criterion-support ratio therefore does not explain which
diagnosis will be selected as primary.

Together, these findings show that candidate ranking, primary-diagnosis
selection, and complete diagnosis-set construction are related but
different problems. They also show that broad criterion compatibility
does not provide enough information to compare several plausible
diagnoses.

The results locate benchmark disagreements in the visible system
outputs. They do not determine whether the system or the dataset
reference is clinically correct, and they do not prove that one system
component alone caused the disagreement.

\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:discussion}

The results support a stage-wise interpretation of HiED rather than a
claim that HiED is the most accurate method. TF--IDF with logistic
regression has the strongest same-source performance in the main
internal comparison. Single and HiED have almost the same Top-1
Accuracy.

HiED's main value is therefore not a higher final accuracy score. Its
value is that it keeps the Top-3, criterion states,
criterion-compatible set, and final primary diagnosis for each case.
These outputs make it possible to examine candidate formation,
criterion checking, and primary diagnosis selection separately.

The internal and external results show the same broad pattern. A
reference diagnosis can already appear in the Top-3 and pass the
criterion rules, while another diagnosis is still selected as the final
primary diagnosis. This is the largest benchmark-disagreement group in
both synthetic datasets.

\section{Interpreting Compatibility and Primary Diagnosis Selection}
\label{sec:discussion-primary-selection}

Candidate formation, criterion checking, and primary diagnosis selection
answer different questions.

Candidate formation asks which diagnoses should remain under
consideration. Criterion checking asks whether the transcript supports
each diagnosis under its own rule. Primary diagnosis selection asks
which diagnosis best explains the case after the possible diagnoses are
compared.

These questions are related, but they are not the same. Several mental
disorders may fit the same incomplete transcript. Poor sleep, fatigue,
poor concentration, low mood, worry, and reduced daily function can
support more than one diagnosis. A diagnosis may therefore pass its own
criterion rule without being the best primary diagnosis.

This difference is clear in the results. The internal
criterion-compatible set contains a median of six of the fourteen
configured diagnoses. The external set has the same median size.
Criterion checking usually keeps the reference diagnosis, but it also
keeps several alternatives.

The overall proportions of met, not met, and insufficient-evidence
states are also similar between cases with a matching final primary
diagnosis and cases with a primary-diagnosis disagreement. A simple count
or proportion of supported criteria therefore does not provide enough
information to choose among several compatible diagnoses.

The 272 internal cases and 225 external cases in which a reference
diagnosis appears in both the Top-3 and the criterion-compatible set but
is not selected as primary should be interpreted carefully. They show
that candidate availability and criterion compatibility do not uniquely
determine the final primary diagnosis.

This supports the hypothesis that primary-diagnosis selection may be a
bottleneck under the tested HiED setting. It does not establish a causal
or clinically validated bottleneck, prove that finalization alone caused
every disagreement, or show that the dataset reference is the only
clinically correct primary diagnosis.

The Chapter~\ref{ch:error} analysis shows another important distinction.
In 109 internal cases, the final primary diagnosis matches a reference
diagnosis, but the complete diagnosis set does not match. Primary
diagnosis selection and complete diagnosis-set construction should
therefore be treated as two separate tasks.

\section{Why the Tested Selection Methods Did Not Improve}
\label{sec:discussion-selection-strategies}

Direct-Answer and Nominate-then-Select provide the clearest comparison
of two final selection rules because they use the same Top-3, criterion
states, and criterion-compatible set.

Nominate-then-Select gives priority to a highly ranked diagnosis that
also passes its criterion rule. However, several diagnoses often pass
their rules at the same time. The compatible set does not show which
diagnosis should be primary. As a result, the rule can replace a correct
Direct-Answer choice with another diagnosis that is also compatible.

Deterministic fusion has the same basic limitation. It uses the
proportion of met criteria as its main selection signal. However, the
criterion-state analysis shows that this proportion is similar between
cases with matching and non-matching final primary diagnoses. A higher
met ratio does not necessarily mean that one diagnosis explains the case
better than the other candidates.

Pairwise comparison and debate attempt to compare candidates more
directly. Self-consistency attempts to reduce variation by repeating the
Diagnostician and using voting. None of these methods provides a clear
and stable improvement over Direct-Answer.

One reason is that all tested methods still use the same fixed
transcript. Additional model calls can reorganize or reinterpret the
available information, but they cannot add facts that are missing from
the transcript.

For example, the transcript may not clearly state:

\begin{itemize}
    \item when the symptoms began;
    \item which symptoms appeared first;
    \item whether similar episodes occurred before;
    \item whether another medical or substance-related cause is present;
    \item whether anxiety is part of a mood episode or a separate
    disorder; or
    \item which syndrome explains most of the patient's current
    impairment.
\end{itemize}

When these facts are missing, repeating the same reasoning process may
not resolve the disagreement.

The negative results do not show that all future selection methods will
fail. They show that the tested rules, comparisons, debates, and voting
methods did not reliably turn candidate availability into better Top-1
Accuracy.

The results suggest that improving primary diagnosis selection may
require two different changes. First, the system may need a better way
to compare candidates directly. Second, it may need a way to identify
and obtain information that is missing from the transcript.

\section{Relation to Existing Systems and Baselines}
\label{sec:discussion-related-systems}

HiED is not the first psychiatric system to use multiple agents,
diagnostic criteria, or intermediate diagnostic outputs. Interactive
systems such as MIND, WiseMind/ProAI, MAGI, and DSM5AgentFlow use
multi-stage or criteria-guided workflows and can ask more questions
during the assessment
\citep{li2026mind,wu2026wisemind,bi2025magi,ozgun2025dsm5agentflow}.

This interactive setting is different from the setting used in this
thesis. An interactive system can collect new evidence when symptom
duration, timing, or illness history is unclear. HiED receives one fixed
transcript and cannot ask follow-up questions.

Fixed-record systems such as MentalSeek-Dx and MoodAngels also organize
diagnostic reasoning around symptoms, evidence, or diagnostic criteria
\citep{sun2026mentalseek,xiao2025moodangels}. Rule-based systems make
their diagnostic rules easier to inspect
\citep{kim2025clp}. However, checking each diagnosis separately does not
automatically solve the comparison among several compatible diagnoses.

The narrower contribution of HiED is therefore not the first use of
agents or diagnostic criteria. Its contribution is a stage-wise
evaluation framework that keeps the main outputs for the same cases.
This allows the study to distinguish:

\begin{itemize}
    \item a reference diagnosis that is missing from the Top-3;
    \item a reference diagnosis that is ranked but does not pass the
    criterion rule; and
    \item a reference diagnosis that is ranked and compatible but is
    not selected as primary.
\end{itemize}

The TF--IDF result also needs careful interpretation. TF--IDF with
logistic regression is a strong baseline when its training and test
cases come from the same dataset. This shows that dataset wording and
label patterns contain strong predictive signals.

However, its performance falls when the training and evaluation
datasets are changed. The decrease appears in both transfer directions,
although the size of the decrease is different. Strong same-source
performance should therefore not be treated as evidence of stable
performance across data sources.

This result does not reduce the importance of the TF--IDF baseline. It
shows that simple lexical models can be difficult to beat when the
training and test data share the same language patterns. At the same
time, it shows why same-source accuracy alone is not enough to judge
transfer.

HiED does not prove better cross-dataset generalization than TF--IDF.
The MDD-5k experiment shows that the same stage-wise questions can be
applied to a second synthetic dataset. It does not show that HiED has a
stable accuracy advantage across datasets.

\section{Implications for Auditable Decision Support}
\label{sec:discussion-set-scope-transfer}
\label{sec:discussion-clinical-implications}

In this thesis, auditability means that the system outputs can be saved
and checked after the diagnosis is produced. It does not mean that the
outputs are clinically correct.

HiED keeps several types of information that are not available from one
final diagnosis alone:

\begin{itemize}
    \item the Top-3 possible diagnoses;
    \item criterion states for each configured diagnosis;
    \item information marked as insufficient;
    \item the criterion-compatible set; and
    \item the final primary diagnosis.
\end{itemize}

These outputs allow a reviewer to see whether a diagnosis was never
considered, failed the criterion check, or remained possible but was not
selected as primary. This is useful for analysis and review.

However, the criterion states and evidence notes are still generated by
the model. They may contain incorrect judgments or unsupported evidence.
Being able to inspect an output is not the same as showing that the
output is correct.

The results suggest two main directions for future decision support.

The first direction is to move from independent confirmation to direct
comparison. Instead of only asking whether diagnosis A and diagnosis B
each pass their own rules, the system should represent why A should be
preferred over B. Useful comparison information may include:

\begin{itemize}
    \item the order in which symptoms appeared;
    \item the course and duration of each syndrome;
    \item previous episodes;
    \item other possible causes;
    \item which diagnosis explains more of the current symptoms; and
    \item whether a second diagnosis should be treated as a comorbidity
    rather than the primary diagnosis.
\end{itemize}

The second direction is to handle missing information more directly.
When the transcript does not contain enough evidence, forcing one
primary diagnosis may be inappropriate. A future system could state
which distinction remains unresolved, propose a focused follow-up
question, or return an insufficient-information or referral result.

These are design implications from the current findings. They were not
tested as completed methods in this thesis.

The clinical value of the recorded outputs also remains unknown. The
present experiments do not show whether the outputs improve clinician
accuracy, speed, safety, or confidence. These questions require
clinician review and evaluation on natural psychiatric data.

\section{Chapter Summary}

The discussion supports four main interpretations.

First, criterion compatibility and primary diagnosis selection are
different tasks. A diagnosis can pass its own criterion rule without
being the best primary diagnosis.

Second, the tested selection methods did not provide a clear improvement
because they either relied on broad compatibility signals or repeated
reasoning over the same incomplete transcript.

Third, the strong same-source TF--IDF result shows that dataset-specific
wording and label patterns are highly predictive. Its cross-dataset
decrease shows that this advantage should not be treated as stable
performance across data sources.

Fourth, HiED's main contribution is not higher final-label accuracy. Its
contribution is to keep the main diagnostic outputs separate so that
candidate formation, criterion checking, and final selection can be
examined for the same cases.

The results suggest that future systems should compare competing
diagnoses more directly and should recognize when important information
is missing. These conclusions apply to the tested synthetic datasets and
do not establish clinical effectiveness.

\chapter{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{ch:limitations}

This chapter explains what the reported results can and cannot support.
The main limitations concern the meaning of the benchmark labels and
measures, the comparison among complete systems, the use of synthetic
data, statistical uncertainty, and the absence of clinical evaluation.

\section{Measurement and Interpretation Limitations}

The computational results measure agreement with dataset reference
diagnoses. They do not measure agreement with independently reviewed
clinical diagnoses.

Some cases contain more than one reference diagnosis. Top-1 Accuracy is
counted as correct when the final primary diagnosis matches any
reference diagnosis after parent-label mapping. This scoring rule is
useful for benchmark evaluation, but it does not show that the matched
diagnosis is the unique clinical primary diagnosis.

The evaluation measures also describe different outputs. Top-1
Accuracy evaluates the final primary diagnosis. Top-3 Accuracy evaluates
a ranked candidate list and is reported only for methods that produce
such a list. Exact Match and F1 evaluate the complete diagnosis set and
are affected by how many diagnoses a method produces. These measures
should not be combined into one general accuracy claim.

The parent-label mapping changes the level of evaluation. F41 and F43
subtypes are mapped to their parent labels. A prediction may therefore
be correct at the parent level while differing from the reference at the
subtype level.

The \emph{Others} label creates another limit. It is used when the
reference diagnosis falls outside HiED's configured output space. HiED
cannot produce this label. Results on the checker-eligible cases
therefore describe only cases whose reference diagnoses are covered by
the configured Criterion Checkers. They are not results for the full
evaluation population.

The stage-wise indicators also require careful interpretation.
$D_3$, $I$, and $S$ show whether a reference diagnosis appears in the
Top-3, enters the criterion-compatible set, and is selected as the final
primary diagnosis. They are related output views, not independent or
causal stages.

The largest observed disagreement group contains cases in which a
reference diagnosis appears in both the Top-3 and the
criterion-compatible set but is not selected as primary. This is an
observed primary-diagnosis selection pattern. It does not prove that the
finalization component alone caused the disagreement or that primary
diagnosis selection is the largest clinical problem.

Criterion compatibility has a further measurement limit. The criterion
states are generated by the model, and the compatible set is produced by
study-specific rules. The datasets do not provide clinician-reviewed
criterion states for comparison. Errors in evidence extraction,
criterion judgment, criterion wording, or rule design may therefore
change the compatible set and the resulting stage-wise groups.

For these reasons, the framework can show where a disagreement becomes
visible in the system outputs, but it cannot show why the disagreement
occurred or which diagnosis is clinically correct.

\section{Experimental and Statistical Limitations}
\label{sec:model-comparison-limitations}
\label{sec:statistical-reproducibility-limitations}

The main system comparison evaluates complete methods. TF--IDF with
logistic regression, Single, and HiED use the same evaluation cases and
parent-label mapping, but they differ in training, retrieval, prompts,
model calls, and output format. A performance difference cannot be
assigned only to the use of multiple agents.

The retrieval comparisons are more controlled because the settings are
compared within the same architecture. However, Global Top-5 and
parent-balanced retrieval still differ in similarity, label
composition, number and order of examples, and prompt length. Their
difference should therefore be interpreted as a comparison of two
complete retrieval settings rather than the isolated effect of label
balance.

Single and HiED also select their retrieval settings separately on the
validation set. Their main configurations are therefore not matched on
retrieval.

The model-based components in HiED should not be treated as independent
experts. They use the same transcript and may share the same model
family, language patterns, and failure modes. Agreement between two
agents or two paths does not provide independent confirmation.

The DA and NtS comparison is the clearest comparison of two final
selection rules because both methods use the same Top-3, criterion
states, and criterion-compatible set. However, this experiment tests
only one NtS rule. It does not show that every criterion-based selector
will fail.

DA may also produce an optional comorbid diagnosis, while NtS produces
one reselected primary diagnosis. Their Top-1 results can be compared
directly, but differences in Exact Match and F1 may also reflect the
different number of diagnoses produced.

The other selection methods provide less controlled comparisons.
Pairwise comparison and debate add model calls and new prompts.
Self-consistency changes the sampling process and inference amount.
Their results compare complete methods rather than the isolated effect
of one final selection step.

The model-size analysis is limited to the tested Qwen3 models. It does
not show that the same pattern will hold for other model families,
training methods, or serving environments.

The statistical results describe uncertainty over the evaluated cases
under the fixed system outputs. They do not include uncertainty from
dataset construction, reference-label validity, prompt design, model
choice, retrieval choice, or synthetic dialogue generation.

A confidence interval that includes zero does not prove that two methods
are equal. It only means that the available cases do not provide clear
evidence of a difference. A confidence interval that excludes zero also
does not identify one causal system component when several parts of a
method changed together.

The criterion-state analysis contains 77,998 diagnosis-by-criterion
judgments. These judgments are grouped within cases and diagnoses and
should not be treated as independent samples. The analysis therefore
summarizes each case before comparing groups and remains descriptive.

Some analysis groups are also small. In particular, the group in which
a reference diagnosis appears in the Top-3 but not in the
criterion-compatible set contains only twelve internal cases. This group
cannot support stable diagnosis-specific conclusions.

Finally, some supporting methods are available only through summary
results rather than complete case-level predictions. New paired tests,
case-transition analyses, or claims about which individual cases changed
cannot be made for those comparisons.

\section{Data and Clinical Generalizability Limitations}

LingxiDiag-16K and MDD-5k contain synthetic Chinese psychiatric
dialogues. They support controlled and repeatable experiments, but they
do not represent natural psychiatric consultations.

Synthetic dialogues may contain more regular wording, clearer symptom
cues, less uncertainty, fewer unrelated details, and simpler illness
histories than real interviews. Similar findings across two synthetic
datasets may therefore reflect shared properties of synthetic data
rather than general properties of psychiatric diagnosis.

The evaluated dialogues are mainly written in Simplified Chinese. They
do not represent spontaneous Traditional Chinese conversations in
Taiwan, code-switching, Taiwanese expressions, local clinical
documentation, or hospital coding practice.

The internal held-out set was sampled from the same LingxiDiag source
used for development. The held-out cases were excluded from baseline
training and retrieval, but they still share the same generation
process, writing style, and label distribution. A full semantic
near-duplicate analysis was not performed. The internal result is
therefore a same-source held-out result rather than independent external
validation.

The validation set was used for prompt development, rule refinement,
sensitivity analysis, and retrieval selection. It should be treated as
development data rather than an independent performance estimate. The
different retrieval ordering between validation and held-out results
also shows the uncertainty of selecting one setting from a single
validation split.

MDD-5k provides a second synthetic data source. It shows that the same
stage-wise analysis can be applied to another synthetic dataset, but it
is not a clinical cohort. The external results do not establish
performance on natural patient interviews or in a different hospital.

The cross-dataset TF--IDF experiment shows that strong same-source
performance can depend on dataset-specific wording and label patterns.
The result does not prove that TF--IDF can never transfer, and it does
not prove that HiED generalizes better. It only shows that same-source
accuracy should not be treated as evidence of stable performance across
data sources.

No psychiatrist review was completed within the thesis period. The
present thesis therefore contains no clinician-derived evaluation of
the dataset labels, criterion states, compatibility rules, system
diagnoses, or evidence notes.

The proposed single-psychiatrist review described in
Section~\ref{sec:clinical-evaluation-plan} remains future work. Even when
completed, a review of twenty selected synthetic cases by one
psychiatrist would provide only a preliminary check of benchmark-label
alignment. It would not establish dataset-wide label validity, clinical
diagnostic accuracy, or clinical validation of HiED.

The thesis therefore does not show that HiED is effective in Taiwanese
psychiatric practice or in any other clinical setting.

\section{Reproducibility and Deployment Boundaries}
\label{sec:clinical-deployment-boundaries}

Reproducing a reported result requires the same data split, label
mapping, diagnostic scope, model and prompt setting, retrieval method,
output definition, eligible population, and statistical procedure.

A Top-3 result calculated from a different type of output is not the
same measure. A compatibility rate calculated with a different set of
Criterion Checkers or a different denominator is also not the same
result. Similar numerical values should not be treated as the same
experiment unless these conditions match.

Some supporting analyses were preserved only as aggregate summaries.
This limits later paired analysis and case-level review. The main
conclusions therefore rely on the comparisons for which the required
case-level outputs and denominators are available.

HiED also lacks several functions that would be necessary for clinical
use. It does not have a clinically validated method for:

\begin{itemize}
    \item detecting an unknown or out-of-scope diagnosis;
    \item abstaining when the available information is insufficient;
    \item referring a case for further review;
    \item calibrating diagnostic confidence;
    \item asking focused follow-up questions; or
    \item monitoring performance after deployment.
\end{itemize}

The \emph{Others} class is used only for evaluation and cannot be
produced by the current system. The system may therefore still return an
in-scope diagnosis when the clinically relevant diagnosis is outside its
configured scope.

The experiments do not evaluate treatment decisions, patient outcomes,
clinician workload, workflow efficiency, subgroup fairness, privacy,
safety, or harm. They also do not show that the recorded outputs improve
clinician decisions.

Before clinical use, the system would require independently reviewed
natural clinical data, prospective workflow and safety studies, privacy
and data-governance controls, clear responsibility for human review,
monitoring for changes in data and model behavior, and regular
revalidation.

HiED should therefore be interpreted as an offline research and review
framework rather than a clinically validated diagnostic system.

\section{Chapter Summary}

The completed evidence supports three limited claims.

First, the thesis provides a same-source comparison of several methods
on a fixed synthetic held-out set.

Second, HiED makes candidate formation, criterion checking, and final
primary diagnosis selection separately visible on two synthetic Chinese
dialogue datasets.

Third, the cross-dataset TF--IDF analysis shows that strong same-source
lexical performance is sensitive to the data source.

The evidence does not establish the clinically correct diagnosis, the
causal effect of one system component, stable performance across
hospitals or patient populations, effectiveness in Taiwanese practice,
or readiness for clinical deployment.

Stronger claims require clinician-reviewed natural data and controlled
prospective clinical evaluation.

\chapter{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Summary of Findings}

This thesis proposed HiED, a hybrid, evidence-grounded multi-agent
framework for differential diagnosis from Chinese psychiatric interview
transcripts. HiED separates the diagnostic process into a diagnosis path
and a criterion-checking path. It keeps the Top-3, criterion states,
criterion-compatible set, and final primary diagnosis as separate
outputs.

The internal results do not show that HiED has the highest final
diagnosis accuracy. TF--IDF with logistic regression has the strongest
same-source performance in the main internal comparison. Single and HiED
have almost the same Top-1 Accuracy, at 0.517 and 0.518.

The separate TF--IDF transfer experiment also shows that strong
same-source performance does not remain stable when the training and
evaluation datasets change. This result does not show that TF--IDF can
never transfer. It shows that its performance depends strongly on the
data source.

HiED provides a different type of value. Its Top-3 Accuracy is 0.802,
while its Top-1 Accuracy is 0.518. This means that a reference diagnosis
is often already present among the leading candidates but is not selected
as the final primary diagnosis.

Among the 915 checker-eligible internal cases, the largest disagreement
group contains 272 cases. In these cases, a reference diagnosis appears
in both the Top-3 and the criterion-compatible set, but another diagnosis
is selected as the final primary diagnosis.

The same form of disagreement is also observed on MDD-5k. Among the 878
checker-eligible external cases, 225 cases contain a reference diagnosis
in both the Top-3 and the criterion-compatible set without selecting it
as the final primary diagnosis. The exact rates differ between the two
datasets, but the same stage-wise question remains useful.

The tested primary-diagnosis selection methods do not provide a clear
and reliable improvement over Direct-Answer. Nominate-then-Select,
deterministic fusion, and debate reduce Top-1 Accuracy. Pairwise
comparison and self-consistency do not show a stable improvement.

These results support two main contributions.

First, HiED provides a two-path framework that keeps candidate ranking
and criterion checking separate. The system records the Top-3,
criterion states, criterion-compatible set, and final primary diagnosis
for the same case.

Second, this thesis introduces a stage-wise evaluation method based on
$D_3$, $I$, and $S$. The method separately checks whether a reference
diagnosis appears in the Top-3, enters the criterion-compatible set, and
is selected as the final primary diagnosis.

These contributions make benchmark disagreement easier to inspect. They
do not establish the clinically correct diagnosis. All completed
experiments use synthetic Chinese psychiatric dialogues, and no
clinician-derived evaluation was completed within the thesis period.

\section{Answers to the Research Questions}

\begin{description}
[

]

\item[RQ1]
\textbf{How does HiED perform relative to conventional classification
and a Single LLM on the same internal split and parent-label
evaluation?}

TF--IDF with logistic regression has the strongest same-source
performance. Single and HiED have almost the same Top-1 Accuracy.
Therefore, HiED does not show a final-label accuracy advantage over the
main baselines. Its added value is the stage-wise diagnostic information
that is not available from a final diagnosis alone.

\item[RQ2]
\textbf{Where does benchmark disagreement appear across candidate
formation, criterion checking, and primary diagnosis selection?}

A reference diagnosis is often already present in the Top-3 and the
criterion-compatible set. The largest internal disagreement group
contains 272 of the 915 checker-eligible cases, or 29.7\%. In these cases,
another diagnosis is selected as the final primary diagnosis.

This is the largest observed final-primary-output disagreement profile
under the tested HiED setting. It motivates the hypothesis of a
primary-diagnosis selection bottleneck, but it does not prove that
finalization alone caused the disagreement or that the dataset reference
is the only clinically correct primary diagnosis.

\item[RQ3]
\textbf{How does similar-case retrieval affect Single and HiED?}

Global Top-5 retrieval increases Single Top-1 Accuracy from 0.466 to
0.517 on the internal held-out set. The Top-1 differences among the
three HiED retrieval settings are smaller, ranging from 0.510 to 0.524.

Retrieval therefore provides a clearer benefit for Single than for HiED
under the tested settings. The results do not establish one retrieval
method as generally best for HiED.

\item[RQ4]
\textbf{Do the tested primary-diagnosis selection methods improve the
final prediction?}

No tested method provides a clear and reliable improvement over
Direct-Answer. Nominate-then-Select performs worse under all three
internal retrieval settings. Deterministic fusion and debate also perform
below Direct-Answer. Pairwise comparison and self-consistency produce
small or inconsistent differences.

The results show that candidate availability and criterion compatibility
do not provide a simple rule for selecting the final primary diagnosis.

\item[RQ5]
\textbf{Does the stage-wise analysis remain useful on a second synthetic
dataset?}

Yes. The same $D_3$, $I$, and $S$ analysis can be applied to MDD-5k.
The largest external disagreement group has the same form as the largest
internal group: a reference diagnosis is present in both the Top-3 and
the criterion-compatible set but is not selected as the final primary
diagnosis.

However, the exact rates and criterion-compatible inclusion differ
between the two datasets. The external result supports the use of the
same analysis method, not stable numerical performance, better
cross-dataset accuracy by HiED, or clinical generalization.

\end{description}

\section{Future Work}

The single-psychiatrist review described below is the only specific
follow-up protocol defined in this thesis. It was not completed within
the thesis period and provides no clinician-derived evidence in the
present work. The other items are broader directions for future
research.

\paragraph{Conduct a single-psychiatrist review of benchmark-label
alignment.}
\label{sec:clinical-evaluation-plan}
\label{sec:lingxidiag-pilot-review}

A future review will examine twenty selected LingxiDiag cases from the
group with $D_3=1$, $I=1$, and $S=0$. These are cases in which a
reference diagnosis appears in both the Top-3 and the
criterion-compatible set but is not selected as the final primary
diagnosis.

One psychiatrist will first review each transcript without seeing the
dataset reference diagnoses, HiED outputs, or the reason why the case
was selected. For each case, the psychiatrist will record whether the
transcript supports one clear primary diagnosis, only a provisional
diagnosis, or no unique diagnosis. The psychiatrist will also record one
primary diagnosis, up to three differential diagnoses, and the most
important missing information.

After this assessment is fixed, the dataset reference diagnosis will be
shown. The analysis will examine whether the reference diagnosis matches
the psychiatrist's primary diagnosis, appears in the differential
diagnosis, remains plausible but not primary, is not supported, or
cannot be judged because the transcript lacks enough information.

This review will be a preliminary single-expert check of benchmark-label
alignment in selected synthetic cases. It will not establish
dataset-wide label validity, clinical diagnostic accuracy, or clinical
validation of HiED.

\paragraph{Compare candidate diagnoses directly.}

The current Criterion Checkers examine each diagnosis separately. They
mainly answer whether one diagnosis is compatible with the transcript.
They do not fully represent why diagnosis A should be selected instead
of diagnosis B.

Future methods could model direct relations among competing diagnoses.
Useful comparison information may include symptom timing, illness
course, episode structure, exclusionary causes, syndrome dominance,
explanatory dependence, and the relation between a primary diagnosis and
a comorbid diagnosis.

Such a method should also treat primary diagnosis selection and complete
diagnosis-set construction as separate tasks. A matching primary
diagnosis does not guarantee that the full set of diagnoses is correct.

\paragraph{Handle missing information explicitly.}

Some disagreements may remain because the fixed transcript does not
contain enough information. Additional model calls cannot recover facts
that were never recorded.

Future systems could identify the distinction that remains unresolved
and propose a focused follow-up question. They could also allow an
insufficient-information, abstention, or referral result instead of
always forcing one primary diagnosis.

Additional sources such as structured symptom scales, intake forms, or
longitudinal records may also provide evidence that is missing from one
interview transcript. These directions require separate evaluation.

\paragraph{Evaluate natural clinical data in independent future
studies.}

Longer-term evaluation would require natural psychiatric interviews and
independent clinician review. Such work should examine whether the
candidate diagnoses, criterion states, missing-information records, and
final diagnosis are clinically meaningful.

It should also evaluate whether the system affects clinician accuracy,
review time, confidence, safety, or workflow. This work would require a
separate clinical protocol and is outside the scope of the present
thesis.

HiED should therefore be viewed as an offline research and review
framework. Its main contribution is not superior final diagnosis
accuracy. Its contribution is to make candidate formation, criterion
checking, and primary diagnosis selection separately visible so that
benchmark disagreement can be examined in greater detail.

\label{app:supporting}

{\footnotesize
\noindent\textit{TF--IDF baseline configuration:} The model is trained on 13,000 source cases. It uses character-boundary 1--2-grams, at most 10,000 features, \texttt{min\_df=2}, \texttt{max\_df=0.95}, and sublinear term frequency. Twelve class-balanced one-vs-rest L-BFGS logistic-regression classifiers use $C=1.0$ and a 2,000-iteration limit. Their probabilities define the ranked labels. The highest-probability class is always emitted as the primary label, and the second-ranked class is additionally emitted when its probability is at least 0.30; no more than two labels are emitted. Ranked Top-3 uses the three highest class scores independently of this emission rule, whereas Exact Match and F1 use the emitted set.
\par}

The internal held-out set was created by largest-remainder allocation and sampling without replacement with NumPy seed 42. Its ordered case-ID fingerprint is \texttt{317a7633a339ae9a}.

\section{Metric Recalculation and Result Provenance}
\label{sec:metric-audit-boundary}

The 1,000-case output of the validation-selected HiED--DA parent-balanced configuration was independently rescored using the definitions above. The recalculation produced Top-1 Accuracy 0.518000, Top-3 Accuracy 0.802000, Exact Match 0.409000, Macro-F1 0.177577, and Weighted-F1 0.433738. These values reproduce the reported three-decimal results of 0.518, 0.802, 0.409, 0.178, and 0.434.

The same audit found a mean gold-set size of 1.093 and a mean predicted-set size of 1.136. HiED--DA emitted one diagnosis for 864 cases and two diagnoses for 136 cases. These counts confirm that Exact Match, Macro-F1, and Weighted-F1 in the main table are complete-set measures rather than primary-only measures. Primary-only F1 was also calculated as an exploratory check, but it is not substituted for the prespecified set-based F1 values.

For Majority, TF--IDF with logistic regression, and the validation-selected Single Global Top-5 configuration, only the retained aggregate headline results are used in this thesis. The available records do not support new paired case-level, output-overlap, or output-cardinality claims for those rows.

First-listed-label agreement compares the final primary diagnosis only with the first-listed projected gold parent. It is used for the confusion matrix but not for the main result. It may differ from any-gold Top-1 Accuracy when a case contains more than one gold parent label.

\paragraph{Deterministic fusion.}
The rule keeps the DA primary unless that diagnosis is absent from the criterion-compatible set. It then checks compatible alternatives in the Diagnostician Top-3. An alternative replaces the DA primary only when its criterion met ratio is at least 0.20 higher. The diagnosis with the highest eligible ratio is selected. Ties are broken by Diagnostician rank, then the fixed category order, and then the diagnostic code.

\paragraph{Pairwise configurations.}
Pairwise comparison starts with diagnoses whose criterion met ratio is at least 0.50. A diagnosis enters the close comparison cluster when the gap to the next met-ratio value is below 0.10. The pairwise Differential agent compares that cluster. Independent pairwise analysis re-runs the HiED path with deterministic decoding (temperature 0, \texttt{top\_k}=1) and a 1,536-token output budget. Its upstream ranking may therefore differ from the saved DA trace.

The posthoc forced-commit result is different. It replays valid preferences already stored in the matched MDD-5k pairwise trace and adds no model call. It is not a separately executed pipeline, and no matching internal result is reported.

\paragraph{Two-view debate.}
The debate pool is the union of the Diagnostician Top-5 and the criterion-compatible diagnoses. One advocate receives the ranking, reasoning summary, and retrieval gist. The other receives the criterion evidence. The advocates run for at most two rounds. Agreement sets the primary diagnosis; otherwise a judge decides. A case uses at most four advocate calls and one judge call, not counting malformed-output reasks. Decoding uses temperature 0, \texttt{top\_k}=1, and a 1,536-token output budget. The internal and external context windows are 8,192 and 24,576 tokens.

\paragraph{Repeated-generation configurations.}
Self-consistency and confidence-informed self-consistency use $K=3$ and $K=5$ Diagnostician samples, temperature 0.7, \texttt{top\_p}=1.0, a 768-token output budget, and seeds 424200--424204. Standard self-consistency counts each primary vote equally. Confidence-informed self-consistency weights each vote by the reported confidence after clipping it to $[0,1]$; missing confidence receives weight 1. Primary ties are broken by weighted score, raw vote count, earliest sample, and diagnostic code. Ranked lists are combined with weighted Borda-style points over the first five positions. Remaining ties are broken by earliest occurrence and then diagnostic code.
